# Coding for Justice phase-one marketing plan

## Marketing outcome

Recruit three credible design partners and prove one full fairness repair. Phase one is not a campaign for mass attention.

## Positioning

**Category:** a public fairness repair workshop.

**Promise:** Bring one repeatable unfair process. Coding for Justice will listen, map who carries its cost, test a practical repair and publish what worked, failed and happens next.

**Campaign line:** One unfair process. One practical repair. Evidence in public.

**Supporting belief:** You do not have to be loud, fluent, confident or connected to count.

## Priority audiences

| Audience                                               | Need                                                        | Invitation                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Frontline charities and community groups               | Relief from one recurring administrative or digital barrier | Bring one process we can repair together                       |
| People affected by the process                         | Respect, control and practical improvement                  | Help define and judge the repair without publishing your story |
| Engineers, designers, researchers and mentors          | Bounded work with a real adopter                            | Take one clearly owned role in a 90-day repair                 |
| Councils, housing, education and health service owners | Better evidence and a workable intervention                 | Help test a change, then respond to the result                 |
| Funders and civic institutions                         | Transparent experiments with adoption evidence              | Fund maintenance and learning, not attention                   |

## Launch sequence

### 1. Partner-first formation

- Send 20–25 individual invitations.
- Hold six discovery conversations.
- Qualify up to three design partners.
- Select one first repair using the product launch gate.

### 2. Private proof

- Prepare the repair brief, baseline, process map and publication agreement.
- Ask affected contributors and the partner to review a private preview.
- Invite 15–20 trusted reviewers to challenge the scope, evidence and safeguards.

### 3. Public launch

- Publish the homepage, covenant and first real repair brief together.
- Give partners a short newsletter and website pack.
- Hold one facilitated listening room and publish an agreed summary, never raw testimony.
- Open only two invitations: offer a bounded skill and follow the repair.

### 4. Public proof

- Publish discovery, test, fairness review, result and hand-off.
- Publish failed hypotheses as well as improvements.
- Approach wider media only when there is a result worth reporting.

## Channels

### Primary

- Canonical website pages.
- Email digest and RSS.
- Partner newsletters and community mailing lists.
- Small partner-hosted talks and listening rooms.
- Searchable evergreen guides.
- Private preview before public publication.
- GitHub for transparent technical maintenance, not as the public experience.

### Signposts only

- LinkedIn, Mastodon, Bluesky and YouTube can point to the canonical record.
- Publish once, answer genuine factual questions and do not chase reactions.

### Avoid in phase one

- X/Twitter debate cycles and media pile-ons.
- Reddit as a default distribution channel.
- Paid engagement advertising or rage-oriented short video.
- Petition mechanics and reactive commentary.

## Thirteen-week editorial rhythm

Publish one substantive **Workshop Note** each Wednesday. Add one short **Repair Receipt** each Saturday only when capacity allows.

The maintenance rule is intentionally ADHD-friendly: the protected workshop
always identifies one bounded next action and one definition of done. On Tuesday,
the steward may draft Wednesday's note from the public ledger; on Wednesday a
human either edits and approves it, writes a different note, or records that no
update is justified. Missing a week creates a review prompt, not a guilt backlog.

1. Why Coding for Justice is becoming a repair workshop.
2. The Jason Arday presumption and community covenant.
3. The first repair, its boundary and its owner.
4. What we heard, with consent and disagreement preserved.
5. The baseline burden: time, errors, access or abandonment.
6. What others have tried and the limits of that evidence.
7. The repair hypothesis and stop conditions.
8. The prototype or process walkthrough.
9. The fairness and accessibility challenge.
10. What changed after contact with reality.
11. The result: worked, failed or unclear.
12. Maintenance, hand-off and institutional response.
13. Retrospective and the decision about the next repair.

Every Repair Receipt answers:

- What happened?
- What evidence changed?
- What remains unfair?
- Who owns the next step?
- When will it be checked again?

The model may help compress these five answers, but it may not invent progress,
read private submissions, choose the evidence level or publish the result.

## Measures

### North star

One repair is adopted, iterated with an owner, or stopped for an evidence-backed reason.

### Partner funnel

- 20–25 tailored invitations.
- Six discovery calls.
- Three qualified design partners.
- One live repair.

### Process integrity

- Every public record reviewed by an affected contributor and partner.
- Zero unauthorised personal stories.
- Every contribution credited.
- Every repair has a baseline, result, limitation, owner and review date.

### Delivery

- At least ten real pilot users where appropriate.
- One case-specific measure improves, or a clear negative result is published.
- Six reliable contributors.
- At least 70% of accepted bounded tasks are completed.
- No volunteer is routinely expected to exceed four hours weekly.

### Audience quality

- 100–150 relevant email subscribers rather than purchased traffic.
- Ten substantive replies or introductions.
- Five qualified inbound partner enquiries.
- Most useful visits arrive through direct, partner, email, search or referral routes.

## Partner invitation

**Subject: One unfair process we could repair together**

Coding for Justice is opening a small 90-day fairness repair workshop.

We are looking for organisations with one recurring process that creates avoidable difficulty, exclusion or wasted effort for the people they serve. Your team would bring the process owner and three to five people who understand its effects. We would bring facilitated mapping, engineering, research and a bounded test.

Nothing personal would be published without review. The result may be a repair, a better route through an existing service, or evidence that the proposed idea should stop.

Would a 30-minute discovery conversation be useful?

## Publication workflow

`draft → affected-person accuracy review → partner and safety review → fairness check → private preview → explicit approval → public release → live verification → scheduled outcome update`
